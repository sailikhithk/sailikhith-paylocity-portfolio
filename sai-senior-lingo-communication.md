# Sai Senior Lingo & Communication Playbook
**Tone & Style Guide for High-Agency Senior / Staff Engineering Communication**

> Extracted from real production interview feedback, recruiter interactions, and technical screens (including Paylocity 2026-09-11).  
> **Core Rule:** Speak and write like a senior practitioner with real production scars, not an AI chatbot reciting textbooks or a junior engineer trying to prove they studied.

## 1. The Core Philosophy: Senior Practitioner vs. AI Slop

| Dimension | AI Slop / Junior Theorist | Senior / Staff Practitioner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perspective** | Academic, textbook definitions, syllabus coverage. | Lived production trade-offs, failure modes, bounded blast radius. |
| **Recruiter Tone** | Stiff, overly grateful, dumping technical sub-clauses. | Direct, warm, concise, confident, highlighting momentum. |
| **Interviewer Tone** | Explaining *how to code it* immediately. | Framing the problem in 60s: algorithm, invariant, what it prevents. |
| **Past Experience** | Pivoting every question into an unsolicited sales pitch. | Answering strictly and deeply about the exact system queried. |
| **Vocabulary** | "Spearheaded", "leveraged", "comprehensive", "robust", "opportunity". | "Shipped", "benchmarked", "profiled", "decoupled", "isolated". |

## 2. Banned AI Phrases vs. Senior Engineering Lingo

### In Recruiter & Follow-up Emails:

| Never Write (AI Slop) | Write This Instead (Senior Lingo) | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"We had a great, deep technical discussion covering rate-limiting architectures (sliding window vs token bucket), OCP-compliant model runner design with runtime protocols..."* | *"Really enjoyed the session - we dug deep into rate-limiting patterns, model serving protocols, and Delta Lake concurrency."* | Recruiters don't evaluate syntax; laundry lists sound like a prompt completion. |
| *"I also had the opportunity to walk Artem through the interactive prototype..."* | *"Toward the end, I also walked him through the interactive prototype I put together..."* | "Had the opportunity to" sounds like a cover letter generated by ChatGPT. |
| *"showcasing real-time anomaly detection with SHAP explainability and human-in-the-loop governance."* | *"anomaly detection with SHAP explainability and human approval checkpoints:"* | Drop corporate filler like "showcasing" and "governance." |
| *"I am very excited about the mission and architecture of the team and look forward to the next stage of the process!"* | *"Really excited about what the team is building on the platform side - looking forward to hearing from you on next steps!"* | Sounds like an energetic colleague, not an automated template. |
| Stranded bare link on its own line: `[portfolio](https://...)` | Embedded or natural link: `https://...` with a one-line intro. | Clean formatting that looks typed by hand. |

### In Technical Interviews & Explanations:

| Never Say (Junior / AI) | Say This Instead (Staff/Senior) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"We use Presidio to facilitate PII redaction seamlessly."* | *"We tuned Presidio over a 3-tier pipeline (regex + ONNX NER on Triton) to bound P99 latency under 12ms."* |
| *"Delta Lake is better than Parquet because it supports transactions."* | *"Parquet is just an immutable columnar file. Delta Lake wraps it with `_delta_log` and Optimistic Concurrency Control, resolving multi-worker conflicts at commit time."* |
| *"Vector databases make search much faster."* | *"Postgres array scans are $O(N)$ sequential page reads. Vector DBs maintain memory-resident HNSW graphs for $O(\log N)$ approximate nearest-neighbor search, with metadata pre-filtering to prevent cross-tenant leaks."* |
| *"We just wrap it in a try/except so it doesn't crash."* | *"A bare except swallows `MemoryError` and DB timeouts, creating zombie worker pods. We catch specific exceptions, log structured context, and route poisoned payloads to a DLQ [Dead Letter Queue]."* |

## 3. Communication Invariants for All Interactions

### Invariant 1: The Strict Direct-Answer Rule (No Diverting)
* When an interviewer asks: *"How did you handle OOMs at Airbnb?"* or *"Tell me about FacadeDriver"*:
  * **Answer strictly and deeply about Airbnb.**
  * **DO NOT** pivot unprompted to Paylocity, Oracle, or another company.
  * Give the exact technical mechanics, root cause, architectural decision, and measured metric.
  * Pivoting when asked a direct question sounds evasive, defensive, or like a canned sales pitch.

### Invariant 2: The Bracketed Acronym Expansion Rule
* In all written interview materials, technical notes, and code reviews, **always expand acronyms in brackets upon first or critical mention**:
  * `BM25 [Best Matching 25]`
  * `NDCG@10 [Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain at rank 10]`
  * `ROC-AUC [Receiver Operating Characteristic - Area Under Curve]`
  * `PR-AUC [Precision-Recall Area Under Curve]`
  * `RRF [Reciprocal Rank Fusion]`
  * `HITL [Human-In-The-Loop]`
  * `PoLP [Principle of Least Privilege]`
  * `ZDR [Zero Data Retention]`
  * `WORM [Write Once Read Many]`
  * `CDC [Change Data Capture]`
  * `DLQ [Dead Letter Queue]`
  * `SLA [Service Level Agreement]`
  * `FLSA [Fair Labor Standards Act]`
  * `HDL [HCM Data Loader]`
  * `HCM [Human Capital Management]`
  * `OOM [Out Of Memory]`
  * `SOC 2 [System and Organization Controls 2]`
  * `HIPAA [Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act]`

### Invariant 3: The 4-Part Code Review Delivery
When presented with code to review, never just say "this is bad" or rewrite it silently. Deliver in 4 crisp steps:
1. **Locate & Name:** Identify the exact line and antipattern (e.g., mutable default argument, quadratic list scan, f-string SQL).
2. **Mechanism (Why):** Explain why Python/runtime behaves that way (e.g., list bound once at module load, $O(N^2)$ list scan).
3. **What It Prevents:** Name the production outage or business disaster avoided (e.g., cross-tenant cache bleed, 504 gateway timeout, IRS tax rounding audit penalty).
4. **The Clean Fix:** Provide the typed, production-ready replacement.

## 5. Punctuation, Formatting & Cadence Rules
- **No Em-Dashes:** Use standard commas, colons, or parentheses. Em-dashes often flag automated AI generation.
- **Short, punchy sentences:** If a sentence has 3 commas and a semicolon, break it into 2 sentences.
- **Backtick technical terms:** `PostgresSaver`, `asyncio.Queue`, `typing.Protocol`, `is_allowed()`.
